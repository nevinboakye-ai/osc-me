# OSC.ME development recordings: participant information and consent

Draft, 26 November 2026. Plain-language form for internal development only. It is not a research ethics form. If results are ever to be published, new consent under an approved ethics protocol is needed.

## What this is

OSC.ME is an early-stage project, led by Nevin, that is testing whether AI can recognise the steps of a clinical examination from a phone video. You are being asked to take part in practice recordings of a simulated respiratory examination.

## What you would do

- Act as an examining student, or as a practice partner ("patient"), in short scripted runs of about 4 minutes each.
- Partners stay clothed (a vest or sports top). No intimate areas are examined or filmed.
- Two phones film each run. Your face and voice will be recorded.

## What happens to the recordings

- They are stored on Nevin's computer and shared storage that only the project team can access.
- They are sent to Google's Gemini AI service (paid tier) to be analysed. On the paid tier, Google states it does not use the content to improve its products.
- Nevin and the project team watch them to label what happened.
- They are used only to develop and test OSC.ME. They are not shared publicly, put on social media or used in marketing.
- Raw videos are deleted within 12 months at the latest.

## Your choices

- Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time during a session.
- You can ask for your recordings to be deleted at any time, without giving a reason, by contacting Nevin. They will be deleted within 14 days, including from shared storage.
- Saying no has no effect on your studies or your relationship with anyone involved.
